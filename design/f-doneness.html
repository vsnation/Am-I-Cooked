The Doneness Chart: the five bands are the interface

A meat counter, not a lab. Wax paper, counter stamps in butcher blue, and the five doneness bands drawn as cross-sections you get pinned onto — the chart is the hero on every screen, so the whole app is one question: which of these five are you today.

Kept: the five-detent knobKept: the figure in the pan

Why this one could win

Palette
Wax paper #EFE6D2, char #241C18, counter blue #2C4A6B, blood #B22B26, rare #E4907E, chilled green #4F7A5C. The one cool colour is the butcher's stamp ink, which keeps the warm paper from going sentimental.
Type
A heavy slab does the poster shouting — band names, the big weight, buttons — with an old-style serif for the spoken lines and monospace only inside stamps and tags. It reads printed rather than rendered.
Kept from B
The knob becomes the counter's doneness setter, blackened with a rare-pink pointer. The figure in the pan is stamped straight onto the paper as the counter's mark, and returns on the surgery screen with two knives crossed above it.
Structure
The five-band chart sits on three of the four screens, and the score's whole job is to point at one band. Comparison is built into the layout rather than bolted on as a percentile line — which matters, because the percentile is the viral payload.
Trade-off
Clearest explanation of the product to someone who has never seen it, and the chart makes a strong share card. Costs vertical space: the chart plus the scale plus the knob is a lot of furniture before any real data appears.
