The Appliance: doneness is a setting on a machine you can hear click

The kitchen half, built as hardware. Graphite and brushed steel, silkscreen labels, four screws and a knob whose five detents are the five bands. The machine speaks through a vacuum-fluorescent strip in mint green, so the only warm colour left in the device is heat itself.

Why this one could win

Palette
Graphite #1C1E20, enamel #24272A, steel #9AA0A6, cadmium #C4342A, filament #FF9D3D, VFD mint #6FE3B8.
Type
Tight grotesque numerals stamped on the panel, a narrow condensed face for silkscreen labels and band names, and monospace confined to the fluorescent strip — the machine's voice is typographically separate from the machine's labels.
Layout
A control panel, not a page: knob and readout share the top plate, money sits in two recessed gauges, faults print to the strip, and the bottom edge is nothing but physical switches. Cool greys everywhere so heat is the only warm thing on the device.
Risk
Skeuomorphism is a commitment — the knob, screws and inset shadows have to be executed precisely or the whole thing reads as a 2010 throwback.
Trade-off
Best demo theatre by far: the knob turning from IV back to II is the recovery story in one shot. Weakest on text-heavy detail — long roast lines fight the panel grid.
