The Order: you didn't get hacked, you got served

A steakhouse check, and the dish is you. Every bad decision is a line item with a price, the doneness is how the house sent you out, and the total at the bottom is what the year cost. Oxblood leather, foil rule, engraved serif — the tone is a fancy restaurant being very polite about a terrible meal.

Why this one could win

Palette
Oxblood leather #5C1F24, menu card #F3EAD6, ink #1A1412, foil #C9A24A, rare #E0846F, settled green #2F6B48. The card scorches from cream to browned as the meal goes wrong, then cools on the last screen.
Type
A high-contrast engraved serif does the menu voice — house name, doneness, totals — with an old-style serif for anything spoken aloud and monospace confined to the check's small print. Prices are tabular so the bill reads like a bill.
Kept from B
The knob returns as the doneness selector, re-skinned in oxblood with a foil pointer, captioned “how would you like it” — the joke being that you never got to choose. The figure in the pan becomes the house crest at the top of the menu.
Structure
The bill is the information design: every loss is a line item, the live exposure is the one red price, and the total is the year. It converts a security report into the one document everyone already knows how to read.
Trade-off
Funniest of the three and the best share card — a check screenshots beautifully. Weakest on the agent story: back-of-house framing makes the Surgeon a waiter rather than something with real authority.
